🧬 Badeer · intro

The biology: a gene that coordinates pushing and gripping

Cell migration is a four-step process:

  1. Push the front edge forward (lamellipodium).
  2. Anchor the leading edge to the surface.
  3. Generate pulling force.
  4. Release the rear.

The gene we perturb coordinates steps 1 and 2: it links protrusion to substrate adhesion. Without it, the cell can still try to push, but the push and the grip stop happening together.

The marker: GFP fused to Arp2/3, the protein complex that builds the lamellipodium. Brighter pixels at the cell edge = more push machinery being recruited.

Three conditions, 30 cells

  • WT wild type. The gene works.
  • KO knock-out. The gene is fully deleted.
  • KI knock-in. The gene is replaced by a single-amino-acid point-mutation version.
The question: does the KI point mutation behave the same as a complete deletion (KO), or does swapping one amino acid produce a phenotype of its own?

2D epifluorescence, 0.318 µm/px, 60 s/frame, single channel, 16-bit. Two imaging sessions per condition.

How the self-training works

  1. Run v3 on all frames.
  2. Filter: keep frames where mask area ≥ 30% of per-cell median and IoU ≥ 0.5 with previous frame. Drop the 2 worst files (ko1/4 95% empty, ko1/8 50% empty).
  3. Treat the surviving 586 (frame, mask) pairs as pseudo-ground-truth.
  4. Fine-tune cyto3 for 100 epochs on those pairs.

📈 Haili · trajectories

Finding 1: KI cells migrate 35% slower

mean speed (µm/min)
WT (N=10)1.03 ± 0.33
KO (N=10)1.06 ± 0.29
KI (N=10)0.68 ± 0.08

Cohen's d: KI vs WT -1.40, KI vs KO -1.68 (very large effects).

KO migrates at WT speed. Only KI slows down. The point mutation breaks something the complete deletion does not.

Two filters that matter

  • Mask-stability filter: per-pair frames where IoU < 0.3 or mask area collapses are rejected. Caught a fragmentation artefact in wt1/33 that had inflated speed by 5×.
  • Persistence cap at 1.0 (geometric upper bound).

Per-cell trajectories, 30 cells across 6 folders. KI panels (right column) cover noticeably less ground.

Finding 2: KI over-polarises Arp2/3 at the leading edge

lam / cyto intensity ratio
WT1.53 ± 0.29
KO1.47 ± 0.45
KI1.83 ± 0.33

Cohen's d: KI vs WT +0.90, KI vs KO +0.85.

Per cell, brighter pixels inside the mask (above the per-frame median) are treated as lamellipodia. KI cells push more Arp2/3 to the edge than WT or KO.

Combined biology reading: KI cells push the front edge harder (more Arp2/3 polarisation, finding 2) yet move 35% less (finding 1). Effort goes in, locomotion does not come out.

This is what "broken protrusion-adhesion coordination" predicts: the lamellipodium activates, but the cell cannot anchor or generate traction, so the push wastes energy.

Dominant-negative reading: a broken-coordination mutation is functionally more costly than completely deleting the gene.

🛠️ Edward · reusable tools

Drop-in pieces for future projects

  • Adaptive normalize: per-stack p99 detector decides whether to stretch. Fixes low-batch failure without breaking high-batch images. 100% concordance with the dataset's 1/2 folder labels.
  • Self-training pipeline: filter v3 outputs by mask area + IoU, retrain cyto3 on the surviving pairs. No hand-drawn labels required.
  • Mask-stability filter: rejects per-pair frames where IoU < 0.3 or area drops below 30% of per-cell median. Caught the wt1/33 fragmentation artefact.
  • Generic compare_masks.py: IoU, Dice, boundary IoU, Hausdorff 95, centroid distance. Drop in any reference masks (hand-drawn or otherwise) and get a per-frame comparison.
  • Generic plot_trajectories.py: 2×3 panel figure from any trajectories.json. Same script ran on batch 1 and batch 2.

Reproducible: all code, manifests, processed CSV outputs, and the 26 MB fine-tuned weights are in the public repo.

Honest caveats

  • Sample size: N=10 cells per condition per batch. Effect sizes are large but a formal mixed-effects ANOVA with batch as a random effect is still pending.
  • No hand-drawn ground truth: the fine-tuned model learned the v3 consensus shape, not a real reference. compare_masks.py is ready to swap in real annotations when they exist.
  • One stubborn case: ko1/7 still failed at 28.3% empty (30 of 106 frames) on the fresh batch. Worth a manual look: bad recording, or genuine phenotype?
  • Photobleaching: not corrected. Absolute intensities should be read with caution. Ratios (lam/cyto) are partially robust because both populations decay together.

Next steps

  • Mixed-effects model with batch as random effect (deliver p-values, not just effect sizes).
  • Photobleaching correction for absolute intensity.
  • Three-layer split (body / transition / lamellipodia) if biology team wants finer granularity.
  • napari interactive viewer for the biology team to browse cells frame-by-frame.
