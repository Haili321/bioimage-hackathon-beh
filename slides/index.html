BioImage Hackathon BEH

A point mutation that tries harder and fails more

Cell migration analysis pipeline + cross-batch validation
on 60 GFP-Arp2/3 time-lapse recordings.

Haili Yuan, Edward Offord, Badeer Ummat
Warwick CAMDU · 2026-04-29 to 05-01

Setup

  • Dataset: 30 time-lapse recordings, 6,221 frames, 1.4 GB
  • Modality: 2D epifluorescence, GFP-Arp2/3, 0.318 µm/px, 60s/frame
  • Three conditions:
    • WT wild-type, normal genome
    • KO knock-out, gene deleted
    • KI knock-in, point mutation in the same gene
  • Biology: the gene coordinates cell protrusion + cell adhesion. Both KO and KI break the coordination, but in different ways.

Together: KI tries harder, fails more

KI cells push more strongly at the leading edge (Arp2/3 polarisation 1.83 vs 1.5 in WT or KO), yet they migrate 35% slower than either WT or KO. Translation does not match intent.

This is exactly what "broken protrusion-adhesion coordination" predicts:

  • The lamellipodium machinery activates more strongly than normal.
  • But the cell cannot generate traction or anchor the leading edge to the substrate.
  • Effort goes in, locomotion does not come out.

Dominant-negative reading: a broken-coordination mutation is more functionally costly than a complete deletion of the gene.

The KI point-mutation product appears to interfere with the migration machinery rather than simply being absent.

Tools we built (drop-in for future projects)

  • Adaptive normalize: per-stack p99 detector decides whether to stretch. Fixes the low-batch failure without breaking the high-batch images.
  • Self-training pipeline: filter v3 outputs by mask area + per-frame IoU, retrain cyto3 on the filtered pseudo-GT. No hand-drawn labels needed.
  • Mask-stability filter: rejects per-pair frames where the mask area collapses or IoU drops < 0.3. Caught a single-cell artefact (wt1/33) that had inflated migration speed by 5x.
  • Generic compare_masks.py: IoU, Dice, boundary IoU, Hausdorff 95, centroid distance. Drop in any reference masks (hand-drawn or otherwise).
  • Generic plot_trajectories.py: 2x3 panel figure from any trajectories.json.

Reproducible: all code, manifests, processed outputs, and the 26 MB fine-tuned weights are in the public repo.

Honest caveats

  • Sample size: N=10 cells per condition per batch. Effect sizes are large but formal mixed-effects ANOVA with batch as a random effect is still pending.
  • No hand-drawn ground truth: the fine-tuned model learned the v3 consensus shape, not a real reference. compare_masks.py is ready to swap in real annotations when they exist.
  • One stubborn case: ko1/7 still failed at 28.3% empty (30 of 106 frames) on the fresh batch. Worth a manual look: bad recording, or a genuine phenotype?
  • Photobleaching: not corrected. Absolute intensities should be read with caution. Ratios (lam/cyto) are partially robust because both populations decay together.

Next steps

  • Mixed-effects model with batch as random effect (give p-values rather than effect sizes alone).
  • Photobleaching correction.
  • Three-layer split (body / transition / lamellipodia) if biology team wants finer granularity.
  • napari interactive viewer for the biology team to browse cells frame by frame.
